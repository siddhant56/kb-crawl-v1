## Why Does Digitization Happen Slowly in Healthcare?

Healthcare happens to be one of the most data-intensive industries. However, it still remains the least digitally transformed. The reasons are more systematic, structural, and cultural than technical.

**Fragmented Data and System Incompatibility:** Traditional healthcare systems function on largely disconnected platforms that aren’t designed to communicate with each other. From scattered data to strict regulations and limited access to structured but anonymous data needed to train AI models make integrating new-age digital tools in clinical care difficult. When systems fail to connect, clinicians and caregivers become integrators, having to bridge gaps manually that tech must have bridged.

**Regulatory and Compliance Complexity:** New-age digital tools in healthcare must steer clear of complex regulations and data sovereignty laws like HIPAA, FDA, along with institutional procurement cycles. From complex regulatory compliances to legal challenges and cyber vulnerabilities, everything contributes to slowing digital adoption for the healthcare sector.

**Cultural and Organizational Resistance:** Culture, governance and organizational structure are traditional hindrances to digitization in healthcare. Tech for healthcare often fails when facing these non-tech hindrances. Misaligned scoping, organizational mental blocks, and biasedness for in-house solutions often delay adoption.

**Workforce Digital Literacy Gap:** Technology moves at breakneck speed, often faster than clinical staff can adapt. Limited exposure and understanding of new-age tools mean that even well-rounded digital initiatives fail at the implementation stage.

**Cost Constraints and Fear of Cybersecurity:** Small-scale institutions back off in the face of large-scale transformation expenses. Also, healthcare being the industry handling the most sensitive personal info, works in a strong institutional bias fearing data vulnerability in innovation.

Healthcare isn’t away from digitization because of technology. Digital transformation in healthcare with advanced AI-led capabilities have to be viewed in a different lens. Governance, culture and HealthOps need to evolve at pace with tech, or the performance-impact gap will always persist.

## Digital Trends in Healthcare Industry

Technologies have driven the healthcare industry through various innovations and advancements for quite some time. The advent of cutting-edge technologies, custom software development, and mobile healthcare platforms has also significantly pushed the industry.

Due to the presence of these, medical practitioners in the healthcare industry provide enhanced patient care through effective and timely communication. So let us review the latest digital healthcare trends that IT offers for the healthcare industry.

### 1\. Artificial Intelligence

The global healthcare AI market is expected to reach $188 billion by 2030, increasing CAGR of 37% from 2022 to 2030. Several hospitals and physicians are using AI to enhance patient treatment based on the data compiled by medical practitioners. AI in healthcare helps in the best possible ways to predict patient behaviors and creates highly accurate digital health profiles.

### 2\. Blockchain

The decentralized ledger in blockchain facilitates seamless interaction between patients, administrators, healthcare services providers, and insurers. Blockchain helps in verifying health credentials in a privacy-preserving way.

It is finding new suppliers for critical goods. It eliminates risks while simplifying and accelerating supplier onboarding and supplier management.

### 3\. Telehealth

This trend is changing the way patients interact with healthcare providers. A few impactful technologies under telehealth are mobile health apps (mHealth), telemedicine, remote patient monitoring, and video conferencing. Telehealth can be integrated with IoT and wearable devices to manage high-risk patients by tracking their activities and data. To be precise, telehealth is a futuristic solution that encompasses broad aspects of digital health.

### 4\. Medical IoT

Hospitals have great success with IoT devices that do more than just assess patients' health. They are utilized to track the real-time location of medical equipment including wheelchairs, nebulizers, oxygen pumps, and numerous other monitoring devices since they are fitted with sensors.

IoT devices make it possible to monitor patients in real-time, improve care, and guarantee efficient healthcare operations with lower levels of mistake, waste, and system expenses.

### 5\. On-demand Healthcare

An on-demand healthcare application works in the best possible ways to save patients from the hassle of visiting clinics and medical organizations regularly for follow-ups. Plus, this trend is not just user-centric.

With its help, patients can book appointments at home, make payments with the app, and get instant summaries of their medical reports that they can use for future medical consultations.

### 6\. Big Data

The decision to digitize records and the quick advancement of medical technology have opened the door for big data to have a substantial impact on the sector. Big data is used by industries to study their consumers and then customize their goods and services.

Significant data sources in the healthcare sector incorporate patient medical records, findings from physical exams, hospital records, and data gathered by medical testing equipment.
