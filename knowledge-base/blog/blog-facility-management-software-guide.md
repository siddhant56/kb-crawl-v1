## What is Facility Management Software?

Facility management software (aka FMS) is a specialized digital asset designed and built to manage and enhance how physical facilities or amenities are supervised in an organization. This advanced software helps businesses manage their infrastructure space and facility operations, allowing them to plan, deploy, and control all the workflows within the space. Moreover, facility management software solutions minimize downtime, enhance operational efficiency and simplify infrastructure management.

It also helps employees manage jobs like scheduling, communication, repair, and maintenance from a web-based panel, further ensuring the safety, control, and efficiency of the people and assets. As an outcome, a facility management system helps organizations simplify workflows and save considerable effort, money, and time. It’s also somewhat like a warehouse management system (WMS) but offers different features and functionalities.

## What are the Features of Facility Management Software?

FMS streamlines and simplifies the tasks involved in overseeing a building or physical space. Here are some of its key facility management software features:

  * **Asset Management:** Track and maintain a detailed inventory of equipment and furnishings, including service history and warranty information.
  * **Cloud-based Solution:** Cloud-based facility management software eliminates the need of additional hardware-ownership costs.
  * **Work Order Management:** Create, assign, and track work orders for repairs, maintenance requests, and other facility service needs.
  * **Preventive Maintenance:** Schedule and automate routine maintenance tasks to prevent equipment failures and extend asset lifespans.
  * **Space Management:** Optimize space allocation and utilization by creating digital floor plans and tracking occupancy rates.
  * **Inventory Management:** Monitor stock levels of essential supplies and automate the reordering process to avoid stockouts.
  * **Resource Planning:** Helps organizing administrative and labour resources by seamlessly collaborating the workforce working in the office and field, minimizing miscommunication.
  * **Reporting and Analytics:** Generate reports that provide insights into facility performance, identify trends, and support data-driven decision-making.
  * **Vendor Management:** This feature helps simplify activities such as ensuring better service delivery, minimizing vendor-related risks, managing expenses, negotiating contracts, or choosing vendors.
  * **Accessible Contractor Directory** : It helps regularly maintain and update contract details. Moreover, you can easily access the directory to look for expert contractors with ideal skills for specific projects.
  * **Move Management:** This feature simplifies the move process from execution request, which minimizes expenses by preventing needless moves. It also streamlines move planning through higher visibility of space resources.
  * **Lease Management:** It helps store all lease-related details in one place. This includes critical dates, tenant details, payment schedules, and asset-level lease terms.

## Different Types of Facility Management Software

Facility management software (FMS) helps businesses manage and maintain their physical amenities efficiently. There are different types of facility management software, each with its specific functionality. Here are some of the most common types:

**1\. Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS):** CMMS software is used to track and manage the maintenance of assets, such as equipment, buildings, and grounds. It can help to prevent breakdowns, extend the life of assets, and reduce maintenance costs.

**2\. Computer-Aided Facility Management (CAFM) Software:** CAFM software creates and manages digital floor plans and other facility drawings. It can also be used to track the location of assets and to manage space utilization.

**3\. Integrated Workplace Management System (IWMS):** IWMS software is the most comprehensive type of facility management software. It integrates all the features of CMMS, FMS, and CAFM and additional features for managing other aspects of the workplace, such as energy, sustainability, and employee productivity.

**4\. Manufacturing Execution System (MES)** : A Manufacturing Execution System (MES) is a software solution used to manage and supervise the production workflow on the factory premise. It is an intermediary between manufacturing equipment and enterprise-level systems like enterprise resource planning (ERP).

**5\. Space Management Software:** Space management software helps manage a company’s physical space inventory and real estate assets. It also allows better utilize physical spaces, including entire buildings, desks, office spaces, and meeting rooms. Furthermore, these facility software solutions provide tools to organize the space effectively.

**6\. Enterprise Asset Management (EAM):** Enterprise asset management (EAM) combines software, systems, and processes to enhance and supervise different physical equipment and resources in an organization. The solution is ideal for asset-intensive industries like manufacturing and can help with equipment maintenance or control physical asset lifecycles to enhance cost controls and utilization.

## A Step-By-Step Process to Implement Facility Management Software

Facility management software can revolutionize how you handle your physical space. But before diving in, a well-planned implementation is crucial for success. Here's a breakdown focusing on the software development process and key people involved:

### 1\. Assemble Your A-Team

Identifying the right personnel who will interact with the FM solution is the first step. You'll need to assemble an A-Team to ensure a smooth rollout. This includes facility managers who understand the day-to-day needs and will be power users of the system. The IT department's expertise is essential for integrating the software with existing systems and managing user access. Don't forget to involve departmental representatives from maintenance, HR (for space management), and finance (for budget tracking) to ensure the system caters to their specific needs.

### 2\. Software Selection and Development Process

Once you have your team in place, it's time to decide on the software. You have two main paths: explore pre-built facility management software options or consider developing custom software. Off-the-shelf software offers a quicker implementation and is often more budget friendly. Evaluate options based on features, scalability, and user-friendliness to find the best fit for your needs. If your requirements are highly specific, custom development might be the way to go. This provides a tailored solution aligned with your specific business challenges and needs but requires a longer development time and potentially a bit higher costs.

### 3\. Build Your Digital Blueprint

The next step is building your digital blueprint. This involves defining and setting up the system. Here, you'll plan the frictionless migration of existing facility data (e.g., asset lists, maintenance schedules, space allocation) into the new software. Ensure compatibility with other systems you use. Work with the software vendor or your development team to configure the system based on your needs. This might involve customizing workflows, creating user roles, and setting up dashboards.

### 4\. Program and Deploy

Now it's go-time! Launching the software smoothly requires a comprehensive test-out and making sure everything is functional properly. During this stage, you need to meticulously test workflows, processes, and other functionalities to ensure they work as planned. Furthermore, run a security audit and accessibility, identify cross-departmental connections, and establish credentials for authorization access.

### 5\. Normalize Its Usage

The final stage focuses on normalizing software usage within your team. To make the system a habit, incentivize and encourage staff to use it consistently. This could involve gamification, user training sessions, or recognition for early adopters. Alongside, schedule regular reviews to assess the software's effectiveness and gather user feedback. By strategically utilizing their feedback, you can refine workflows and functionalities to ensure the system continues to meet your evolving needs.

## Challenges When Adopting Facilities Management Software

Adopting facilities management software does bring significance to organizations but also comes with challenges. Following are some of the most common challenges organizations might face when leveraging FMS:

  * **Training and Skill Development:** The learning curve for adopting new software can be a tough climb. If your employees are not trained adequately, using the software efficiently might be challenging, which minimizes its overall worth and can make the adoption harder.

  * **Reluctance to Change:** Investors and employees sometimes may resist changing existing workflows or using new technologies due to the fear of losses. Breaking these barriers requires adequate training, seamless communication, and strong leadership.

  * **Data Migration and Quality:** Transmitting data from your existing outdated systems to new, advanced software can sometimes lead to errors, missing information, or data inconsistencies. Ensuring accuracy and data quality during migration is essential to prevent operational setbacks.

  * **Cost of Implementation:** The initial cost of leveraging a facility management solution can be considerable, particularly if customized or comprehensive. Moreover, added expenditure for training, integration, and ongoing maintenance might stretch the budgets, making it vital to analyze the ROI.

  * **Integration with Existing Systems:** Many amenities have legacy systems already in motion, such as inventory management systems, building management systems, or accounting. Hence, integrating facilities management software with these solutions can be time-consuming and challenging and sometimes even need custom configurations or even revisions to a few processes.
