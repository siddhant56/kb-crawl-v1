## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is insurance software development?

Insurance software development is the process of creating custom digital products to handle claims management, policy administration, underwriting, and broker/customer portals for carriers, MGAs, TPAs, and reinsurers. Insurance software systems are tailored to the specific workflows, compliance needs, and integration requirements of insurance companies.

### How long does it take to build an insurance software platform?

Most insurance platforms take 4–6 months for an MVP, 9–15 months for full core systems, and 12–24 months for large legacy migrations. Smaller projects like embedded insurance APIs can be done in a couple of months. With our agile approach, you will see usable features every few weeks instead of waiting for one big bang launch.

### What technologies are used in modern insurance software?

Modern insurance platforms are mostly cloud-native and API-first. You’ll usually see React or similar frameworks for web and portals, backed by services in Node.js, Python/Django, or Java Spring Boot that work on top of PostgreSQL or similar databases. Under the hood, Kubernetes and public cloud (AWS, Azure, or GCP) handle scaling, while AI/ML models help with pricing, fraud, and recommendations.

### How do you connect insurance software with legacy platforms like Guidewire or Duck Creek?

The usual strategy is “wrap, don’t rip.” Instead of ripping out Guidewire or Duck Creek, you expose the useful parts like policy lookups or claims status as APIs and slowly move new features onto a modern stack. Data gets translated from older formats into clean JSON so newer apps can understand it. For a while, both worlds run side by side, and traffic is gradually shifted over once results match, so there’s no service disruption or downtime.

### What compliance and security standards do your insurance platforms support?

At a minimum, our insurance platforms comfortably tick boxes for HIPAA (if you touch health data), GDPR for EU privacy, SOC 2 for security and availability, and whatever local regulator applies Solvency II in the EU. On top of that, payment flows need PCI DSS, and larger insurers often look for ISO 27001 as proof that security is managed properly. In practice, that translates into encryption everywhere, strict access controls, detailed audit logs, and integrations with your monitoring and SIEM tools.

### What outcomes can carriers and MGAs expect from custom insurance software?

Custom insurance software typically clears claims and FNOL evidence within 4–8 hours in peak batches, reduces policy servicing cost by 20–40%, and completes quote-to-bind cycles same-day for many carriers, MGAs, and TPAs. Over a year or two, that typically adds up to a strong ROI and much more freedom to launch new products or tweak business models without fighting the tech.
